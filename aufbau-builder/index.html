THE ELECTRON BUILDING RULES

How to write a configuration

  1. Count electrons. For a neutral atom, the electron total equals its atomic number, Z.
  2. Follow the filling order. Aufbau means “building up.” Electrons occupy available lower-energy orbitals in the basic pattern.
  3. Know each sublevel’s capacity. s = 1 orbital / 2 electrons; p = 3 / 6; d = 5 / 10; f = 7 / 14. One orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins.
  4. Stop at the correct total. If four electrons remain and you reach 2p, write 2p⁴, not 2p⁶.
  5. Check the superscripts. Their sum must equal Z.
Worked example: oxygen, Z = 8

1s² → 2s² → 2p⁴

Place 2 in 1s, 2 in 2s, then the remaining 4 in 2p. Total: 2 + 2 + 4 = 8.

Worked example: iron, Z = 26

1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶

The first 18 electrons match argon. Then fill 4s² and place the remaining six in 3d.

Read the notation

2p⁴: 2 is the principal energy level; p identifies the sublevel; 4 is the number of electrons in that sublevel.

Optional diagonal shortcut: assign l = 0, 1, 2, 3 to s, p, d, f. Lower n + l comes first; if n + l ties, lower n comes first. This is the general Madelung pattern.

Important exceptions: neutral chromium is [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ and copper is [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰. Their observed ground states differ from straight filling-pattern predictions. The atom challenges omit these two elements.

Energy vs. writing order: the 4s/3d energy relationship changes with occupation. Writing 3d before 4s is common in chemistry; this game explicitly requests the Aufbau filling-order display. It does not teach that 4s is always lower in energy than 3d.
